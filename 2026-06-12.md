### [Bipartisan "Great American AI Act" Draft Proposes First U.S. Federal AI Framework](https://fedscoop.com/bipartisan-great-american-ai-act-draft-proposes-new-federal-ai-governance-framework/)
**Source:** FedScoop | **Published:** June 4, 2026
**Summary:** Representatives Jay Obernolte (R-CA) and Lori Trahan (D-MA) released a discussion draft of the Great American Artificial Intelligence Act, covering four pillars: Frontier AI Governance, Workforce, Cybersecurity, and R&D/International Cooperation. The draft would establish a $300 million Center for AI Standards and Innovation inside Commerce and impose a three-year preemption on state-specific AI model-development laws while safety incident reporting and whistleblower protections for AI employees move to the federal level.
**Why it matters:** This is the most substantive bipartisan federal AI bill to date, directly challenging the patchwork of state laws and potentially setting national guardrails for frontier models.

### [MiniMax M3: Open-Weight Frontier Model with 1M-Token Context Debuts](https://the-decoder.com/minimax-m3-open-weight-model-with-a-million-token-context-challenges-proprietary-leaders/)
**Source:** The Decoder | **Published:** June 1, 2026
**Summary:** MiniMax launched M3, the first open-weight model to combine a 1-million-token context window with frontier-tier software engineering scores (59.0% on SWE-Bench Pro, surpassing GPT-5.5 and Gemini 3.1 Pro) and native multimodal input. The core innovation is MiniMax Sparse Attention (MSA), which reduces per-token compute at 1M context to one-twentieth of the prior generation and delivers 15× faster decoding. Open weights and a technical report are scheduled for release on Hugging Face within days.
**Why it matters:** If the open-weight claims hold up under independent replication, M3 collapses the gap between proprietary and open frontier models for long-context agentic coding tasks.

### [Google's TurboQuant Slashes KV-Cache Memory at ICLR 2026](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/current)
**Source:** arXiv / ICLR 2026 | **Published:** June 2026
**Summary:** Google Research unveiled TurboQuant at ICLR 2026, a two-step KV-cache compression algorithm combining PolarQuant vector rotation with a Quantized Johnson-Lindenstrauss compression method. The approach dramatically reduces the memory overhead of long-context inference — one of the chief bottlenecks in running large models on consumer and edge hardware — without meaningful degradation in output quality.
**Why it matters:** Cheaper long-context inference directly lowers the cost of the agentic and retrieval-augmented AI applications that are driving most enterprise AI spending.
