### [Microsoft Build 2026: MAI-Thinking-1 Is First In-House Reasoning Model, Trained Without OpenAI Data](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/02/microsoft-unveils-new-ai-models-lessen-reliance-on-openai-lower-costs.html)
**Source:** CNBC | **Published:** June 2, 2026
**Summary:** At its Build 2026 conference, Microsoft unveiled MAI-Thinking-1, its first proprietary reasoning model built entirely on commercially licensed data with no distillation from OpenAI. The 35B active-parameter sparse MoE model with a 256K context window scores 97.0% on AIME 2025 and claims to match Claude Opus 4.6 on SWE-Bench Pro. Alongside it, Microsoft launched MAI-Code-1-Flash, a 5B-parameter coding model now live in GitHub Copilot across all tiers that uses 60% fewer tokens than comparable models on hard tasks.
**Why it matters:** Microsoft's first fully independent frontier models signal a strategic pivot away from OpenAI dependency and mark a new phase of in-house AI sovereignty for the world's most valuable software company.

### [Trump Signs Executive Order Inviting Voluntary Vetting of Top AI Models for National Security](https://www.washingtonpost.com/business/2026/06/02/trump-ai-executive-order/bc796a3c-5ea4-11f1-9c46-d6211372eede_story.html)
**Source:** The Washington Post | **Published:** June 2, 2026
**Summary:** President Trump signed an executive order directing AI companies to voluntarily submit their most powerful models to government testing up to 30 days before public release. The order also establishes an AI cybersecurity clearinghouse to identify software vulnerabilities in critical infrastructure and creates a classified benchmark for assessing advanced AI cyber capabilities. Crucially, it explicitly prohibits mandatory licensing or pre-clearance requirements.
**Why it matters:** The order is the most significant U.S. federal AI governance action of 2026, threading a needle between national security oversight and avoiding the regulatory burdens that could disadvantage American AI companies against China.

### [EU Council and Parliament Agree to Simplify and Streamline AI Act Rules](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/05/07/artificial-intelligence-council-and-parliament-agree-to-simplify-and-streamline-rules/)
**Source:** Council of the EU | **Published:** May 7, 2026
**Summary:** EU negotiators reached a provisional agreement under the "Digital Omnibus VII" package to significantly amend the AI Act, pushing compliance deadlines for high-risk AI systems back by 16 months (to December 2027) and extending SME exemptions to companies with up to 500 employees. Two new prohibitions were added banning AI-generated non-consensual intimate imagery and CSAM. Final formal adoption is expected in June, with publication in July.
**Why it matters:** The amendments represent the first substantive revision to the EU AI Act since its June 2024 passage, easing near-term compliance burdens while adding sharper teeth around the most harmful AI misuse cases.

### [arXiv: "AI Can Learn Scientific Taste" — LLM Judges Outperform GPT-5.2 and Gemini 3 Pro on Science Evaluation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.14473)
**Source:** arXiv | **Published:** March–June 2026
**Summary:** A new paper proposes training AI systems to evaluate scientific quality using Reinforcement Learning from Community Feedback (RLCF), leveraging citation signals as supervision. Their "Scientific Judge" model substantially outperforms GPT-5.2 and Gemini 3 Pro on Sci-JudgeBench and generalizes to unseen fields and future-year holdouts, demonstrating that scientific taste is a learnable objective rather than a uniquely human trait.
**Why it matters:** If AI can reliably evaluate research quality, it could dramatically accelerate peer review, grant allocation, and automated scientific discovery pipelines.

### [MAGIC: Training-Free Coreset Selection Boosts Vision-Language Model Efficiency](https://arxivtldr.org/weekly)

### [Colorado Scales Back Landmark AI Liability Law](https://ground.news/interest/ai)
**Source:** Ground News / industry reports | **Published:** June 3, 2026
**Summary:** Colorado's legislature moved to significantly roll back its 2024 AI liability law — one of the first U.S. state-level AI regulations — following lobbying from tech companies who argued the statute's broad developer liability provisions would chill innovation and drive AI businesses out of the state. The revised bill narrows the scope of covered systems and raises the bar for proving harm.
**Why it matters:** Colorado's retreat may signal that aggressive U.S. state-level AI regulation faces a difficult path, potentially leaving the federal executive order approach as the primary domestic governance mechanism in the near term.
