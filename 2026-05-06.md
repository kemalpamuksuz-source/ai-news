### [Pentagon Freezes Out Anthropic, Signs Classified AI Deals With Eight Rivals](https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2026/05/01/pentagon-ai-deals-microsoft-amazon-google-classified-military/)
**Source:** The Washington Post | **Published:** May 1–4, 2026
**Summary:** The Defense Department signed agreements with OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, Amazon, Oracle, Nvidia, SpaceX, and Reflection AI to embed AI into classified military networks for logistics, analysis, and large-scale data processing. Anthropic was excluded after refusing to grant the Pentagon unrestricted access to Claude for autonomous weapons and mass domestic surveillance — the administration subsequently designated Anthropic a supply-chain risk and ordered a six-month phase-out.
**Why it matters:** The exclusion of a major frontier lab from classified defense contracts sets a precedent for how AI safety policies can have direct geopolitical and commercial consequences.

### [OpenAI Open-Sources Symphony: Coding Agent Orchestration at Scale](https://openai.com/index/open-source-codex-orchestration-symphony/)
**Source:** OpenAI | **Published:** May 5, 2026
**Summary:** OpenAI released Symphony, an open-source spec (published as a SPEC.md on GitHub) that turns Linear project boards into a control plane for Codex coding agents. Symphony monitors a task board, assigns an agent workspace to each open ticket, restarts crashed agents, and runs until every task is merged. OpenAI reported a sixfold increase in merged pull requests on internal teams in Symphony's first three weeks, with reference implementations shipped in Elixir, TypeScript, Go, Rust, Java, and Python.
**Why it matters:** Symphony shifts the agentic coding paradigm from human-supervised sessions to fully autonomous, project-management-driven execution — and doing it via an open spec rather than a closed product accelerates ecosystem adoption.

### [Google's TurboQuant Slashes KV-Cache Memory Overhead for Long-Context Models](https://iclr.cc/)
**Source:** ICLR 2026 / Google Research | **Published:** May 2026
**Summary:** Google's research team unveiled TurboQuant at ICLR 2026, an algorithm that compresses the KV-cache memory footprint of large-context models using a two-step pipeline: PolarQuant vector rotation followed by Quantized Johnson-Lindenstrauss compression. The technique allows models with multi-million-token context windows — like Gemini 3.1 Ultra's 2M-token window — to run significantly more efficiently without quality degradation.
**Why it matters:** KV-cache memory is the primary bottleneck limiting long-context inference at scale; a practical compression method makes frontier-length contexts economically viable for enterprise deployment.
