### [White House Issues Executive Order on AI Innovation and Security](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/06/promoting-advanced-artificial-intelligence-innovation-and-security/)
**Source:** The White House | **Published:** June 2, 2026
**Summary:** President Trump signed "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security," establishing a voluntary federal framework for frontier AI model development, a public-private AI cybersecurity clearinghouse to detect software vulnerabilities at scale, and a secure early-access program for government review of advanced models. The order explicitly prohibits any mandatory licensing or pre-clearance requirements for AI model releases, framing deregulation as essential to U.S. competitiveness.
**Why it matters:** The EO accelerates a federal preemption strategy that is already forcing state AI laws to be rewritten — Colorado's landmark AI Act was effectively gutted within weeks of similar federal pressure.

### [MiniMax M3: Open-Weight Model With 1M Context Challenges Proprietary Leaders](https://the-decoder.com/minimax-m3-open-weight-model-with-a-million-token-context-challenges-proprietary-leaders/)
**Source:** The Decoder / MiniMax | **Published:** June 1, 2026
**Summary:** Chinese AI lab MiniMax released M3, the first open-weight model to combine a one-million-token context window, native multimodal input (image, video, computer use), and frontier coding performance in a single architecture. ### [Colorado Repeals Landmark AI Act, Replaces It With Disclosure-Only Framework](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/colorado-ai-law-in-flux-comprehensive-replacement-bill-signed-after-federal-court-blocks-predecessors-enforcement/)
**Source:** McDermott Will & Emery / Alston & Bird | **Published:** May 14, 2026
**Summary:** Colorado Governor Jared Polis signed SB 26-189, repealing the state's first-in-the-nation AI liability law (SB 24-205) before its June 30 effective date. The replacement strips algorithmic-discrimination protections in favor of a narrower ADMT disclosure framework taking effect January 1, 2027. The change followed a federal court stay of the original law — obtained after xAI filed a constitutional challenge backed by a DOJ intervention — and direct White House criticism naming the act as a model that would "force AI models to produce false results."
**Why it matters:** The collapse of Colorado's AI Act under federal pressure confirms that the U.S. regulatory landscape for AI is consolidating around a permissive federal baseline, with state-level protections increasingly unenforceable.
