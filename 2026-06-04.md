### [Trump Signs AI Executive Order on Innovation and Security](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/06/promoting-advanced-artificial-intelligence-innovation-and-security/)
**Source:** The White House / NPR | **Published:** June 2, 2026
**Summary:** President Trump signed an executive order titled "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security," establishing a voluntary pre-release review framework for frontier AI models and creating an AI Cybersecurity Clearinghouse to coordinate vulnerability scanning and remediation. The order explicitly prohibits mandatory government licensing or pre-clearance requirements for AI development while inviting companies to voluntarily submit frontier models for government testing up to 30 days before public release.
**Why it matters:** The order sets the tone for U.S. federal AI governance — pro-innovation and voluntary-first — while escalating tensions with states pushing their own mandatory AI rules.

### [Colorado Repeals and Replaces Its AI Act, Delays Enforcement to January 2027](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/colorado-ai-act-amended-and-effective-date-delayed)
**Source:** Hunton Andrews Kurth / Littler | **Published:** May 14, 2026
**Summary:** Colorado Governor Polis signed SB 189, repealing the original Colorado AI Act before its scheduled June 30, 2026 effective date and replacing it with the Colorado Automated Decision-Making Technology Act (CADMA), effective January 1, 2027. The new law abandons EU-style risk-based provisions — including duty-of-care obligations and algorithmic impact assessments — in favor of narrower transparency and disclosure requirements.
**Why it matters:** Colorado's retreat from its comprehensive AI governance model, partly under White House pressure, may signal a national shift away from risk-based AI regulation frameworks.
