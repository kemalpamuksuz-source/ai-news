### [OpenAI Ends Microsoft Exclusivity, Freeing Path for Amazon and Google Cloud Deals](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/27/openai-ends-microsoft-legal-peril-over-its-50b-amazon-deal/)
**Source:** TechCrunch / Bloomberg | **Published:** April 27, 2026
**Summary:** Microsoft and OpenAI renegotiated their foundational partnership, replacing Microsoft's exclusive access to all OpenAI IP with a non-exclusive license through 2032. Microsoft will stop paying a revenue share to OpenAI while OpenAI continues paying a capped revenue share to Microsoft through 2030. The restructuring clears the legal obstacle blocking OpenAI's up-to-$50B deal with Amazon, and lets OpenAI serve products across any cloud provider.
**Why it matters:** This is a seismic shift in the AI cloud landscape — OpenAI is no longer tethered to a single hyperscaler, enabling it to pursue deep partnerships with AWS, Google Cloud, and others simultaneously.

### [David Silver's Ineffable Intelligence Raises $1.1B Seed at $5.1B Valuation](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/27/deepminds-david-silver-just-raised-1-1b-to-build-an-ai-that-learns-without-human-data/)
**Source:** TechCrunch / Bloomberg / CNBC | **Published:** April 27, 2026
**Summary:** London-based AI startup Ineffable Intelligence emerged from stealth with a $1.1B seed round — the largest seed financing ever raised by a European startup — co-led by Sequoia and Lightspeed, with Nvidia and Google participating at a $5.1B valuation. **Why it matters:** Silver's post-human-data approach to AI learning is a fundamental bet against the data-scaling paradigm that has dominated the field, and the record-breaking seed signals investor conviction that RL-based approaches are the next frontier.

### [Stanford HAI Releases 2026 AI Index: 53% Global Adoption, Safety Concerns Rising](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/inside-the-ai-index-12-takeaways-from-the-2026-report)
**Source:** Stanford HAI | **Published:** April 2026
**Summary:** Stanford's annual AI Index found generative AI reached 53% global population adoption within three years — faster than the PC or internet — and organizational adoption hit 88%. On coding benchmarks (SWE-bench Verified), AI performance jumped from 60% to near 100% in a single year; on OSWorld computer-task agents, accuracy rose from ~12% to 66.3%, within 6 points of humans. However, the Foundation Model Transparency Index dropped from 58 to 40 points, and AI data center power demand reached 29.6 GW — equivalent to peak New York State consumption.
**Why it matters:** The Index provides the most comprehensive annual baseline for how fast AI is advancing technically and being adopted globally, while flagging concrete transparency and environmental backslides.

### [Anthropic's Project Glasswing Uses Claude Mythos to Find Zero-Day Vulnerabilities](https://fortune.com/2026/04/07/anthropic-claude-mythos-model-project-glasswing-cybersecurity/)
**Source:** Fortune / The Hacker News / Anthropic | **Published:** April 7–27, 2026
**Summary:** Anthropic restricted its 10-trillion-parameter Claude Mythos model to a curated group of security partners — including AWS, Apple, Google, Microsoft, Cisco, CrowdStrike, and NVIDIA — under Project Glasswing. The model autonomously discovered thousands of zero-day vulnerabilities across all major operating systems and browsers, including a 27-year-old crash bug in OpenBSD and a 17-year-old remote code execution flaw in FreeBSD. The Hacker News reported this week on the gap between finding bugs and patching them at scale.
**Why it matters:** Project Glasswing is the first confirmed case of a frontier AI model being deliberately withheld from the public due to offensive capability risk, while simultaneously being deployed for defensive purposes.

### [Google Unveils 8th-Gen TPUs: Separate Training (8t) and Inference (8i) Chips](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/compute/tpu-8t-and-tpu-8i-technical-deep-dive)
**Source:** Google Cloud Blog / CNBC | **Published:** April 22, 2026
**Summary:** At Google Cloud Next, Google announced two eighth-generation TPUs optimized for distinct workloads: TPU 8t for training and TPU 8i for low-latency inference. The TPU 8i packs 384 MB of on-chip SRAM (3× Ironwood) and scales to 1,152 chips per inference pod delivering 11.6 exaflops of FP8 compute, with 80% better performance-per-dollar over Ironwood. Both chips achieve 2× better performance-per-watt and will be generally available later this year.
**Why it matters:** By splitting training and inference into purpose-built chips, Google is directly challenging Nvidia's GPU dominance and signaling that the agentic-AI era demands radically different silicon architectures.

### [White House National AI Policy Framework Pushes Federal Preemption of State Laws](https://www.alston.com/en/insights/publications/2026/04/ai-quarterly-april-2026)
**Source:** White House / Alston & Bird AI Quarterly | **Published:** April 2026 (framework released March 20)
**Summary:** The Trump Administration's National Policy Framework for Artificial Intelligence, released March 20 and actively debated in April, recommends Congress establish a unified federal AI standard that preempts state laws deemed to "impose undue burdens." It calls for child-safety and age-verification mandates, rejects creating any new federal AI regulator, and instead directs AI governance through existing agencies. The RAISE Act, imposing transparency and safety requirements on frontier model developers, took effect March 19.
**Why it matters:** Federal preemption of state AI laws — if passed — would eliminate the patchwork of state-level rules that currently creates compliance complexity for every AI company operating in the US.
